## Profile

Qiao-Ping Wang is a "Hundred Talents Program" Professor and Doctoral Supervisor at the School of Pharmaceutical Sciences, Sun Yat-sen University. He serves as Vice Director of the Department of Pharmacology (since June 2026) and Director of the Laboratory of Metabolism and Aging, and is a recipient of the Guangdong Distinguished Young Scholars Fund. He received his Ph.D. from Sun Yat-sen University in 2010 (joint training at the London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine, 2008–2010) and completed postdoctoral training at the Neuroscience Division of the Garvan Institute of Medical Research, UNSW, and the Charles Perkins Centre, University of Sydney (2011–2017), focusing on the mechanisms and translation of obesity. He returned to China full-time in October 2017 and joined the School of Pharmaceutical Sciences (Shenzhen), Sun Yat-sen University.

His research addresses the core pathological link of energy intake–expenditure imbalance by building a cross-species pipeline — human genetic data → model-organism screening → mouse functional validation → candidate drug discovery — forming a complete chain from target discovery and mechanistic dissection to drug development for obesity, diabetes and metabolic diseases. He has published more than 50 papers, 25 of them as first or corresponding author (including co-first and co-corresponding authorship), and has led 3 National Natural Science Foundation of China projects and 11 projects in total (national, provincial/municipal and industry), with cumulative funding of approximately RMB 9 million.

## Research Areas

- **Molecular regulation of energy homeostasis and glucose metabolism**: functional validation of obesity GWAS candidate genes; mechanistic dissection of the intake and expenditure regulatory networks.
- **Target discovery and drug development for metabolic diseases**: small molecules, monoclonal antibodies, siRNA and circular mRNA-LNP platforms.
- **Neurobiology of taste perception and feeding behavior**: sweet taste–energy coupling, metabolic effects of non-nutritive sweeteners, and the underlying neural circuits.
- **Functional genomics and AI-assisted drug discovery**: genome-wide CRISPR screens combined with computational virtual screening for integrated target validation and lead discovery.

## Major Research Achievements

### 1. Decoding the energy-intake regulatory network: from "passive taste" to "active sensing" and "metabolic dialogue"

We established a sweet taste–energy coupling model and revealed a bidirectional central mechanism that integrates sweet-taste signals with energy information: when sweet taste and energy are uncoupled, the mismatch drives increased food intake; when energy is sufficient but sweet taste is absent, the body counterintuitively triggers taste sensitization. After establishing his independent group, he further identified the PKA–PGC1α pathway driving taste-neuron sensitization and uncovered a descending "NPY–dopamine–taste neuron" circuit, redefining taste as a dynamic system that actively adapts to energy status. This work was published as first or corresponding author in *Cell Metabolism* (2016, 2017), *Cell Reports* (2020) and *Journal of Genetics and Genomics* (2023), and was highlighted by Nature Research Highlights and commented on by Science Signaling.

Mining obesity GWAS data, we identified the endoplasmic reticulum protein SEC16B and demonstrated, through large-scale Drosophila screening and mouse models, that it regulates glucose homeostasis via cholinergic signaling and insulin exocytosis, revealing a direct molecular dialogue between feeding behavior and glucose homeostasis (*Diabetologia* 2025, sole corresponding author, selected as an Editor's Pick).

### 2. Decoding the energy-expenditure regulatory network: identifying endogenous "brake and accelerator" molecules of thermogenesis

We established two complementary routes: human-genetic-data-driven and physiological-stimulus-driven. On one hand, genetic cohort analysis identified the adipose mitochondrial protein MTCH2 and demonstrated that it blocks UCP1-dependent thermogenesis by stabilizing Bcl-2 and inhibiting lipophagic flux, making it an endogenous suppressor of adipose thermogenesis (*Advanced Science* 2025, last corresponding author). On the other hand, transcriptomic screening identified the calcium channel TRPC6, which selectively activates thermogenesis via the BMPR2–p38 MAPK axis (*Molecular Metabolism* 2026, co-corresponding author). More recently, we identified the adipokine NMI as a linker between inflammation and thermogenesis; NMI transcriptionally represses PPARα/PGC-1α/UCP1 via TLR4/IRF3, and an anti-NMI monoclonal antibody achieved therapeutic validation in obese mice (*Advanced Science* 2026, co-corresponding author).

### 3. Proposing a dual-target "intake–expenditure" strategy and building a target-validation and translation platform

Combining central appetite suppression (metformin) with peripheral thermogenesis (mirabegron) produced 40% greater weight loss than either monotherapy in obese mice, providing proof of concept for dual-network synergistic intervention (*MedComm* 2023, last corresponding author, cover article; granted invention patent). Integrating functional genomic screening with AI-based drug prediction, and using lethal amatoxin as a test case, we identified STT3B and predicted indocyanine green as an antidote (*Nature Communications* 2023, last corresponding author; *Nature Protocols* 2024, last corresponding author), which was featured by Nature News. We further extended circular mRNA and LNP delivery to dry age-related macular degeneration: a single intravitreal injection achieved neuroprotection comparable to 14 consecutive days of NMN injection (*Journal of Controlled Release* 2026, co-corresponding author; patent applied).

### 4. Building a cross-species, full-chain research platform and team

We established a cross-species platform — "human cohort → Drosophila functional screening → mouse validation" — and have identified three key regulators of energy homeostasis; we also built a small-molecule drug discovery platform integrating mechanistic dissection, target discovery and translational application. We maintain long-term collaborations with leading teams in China and abroad, with joint work published in *Cell*, *Nature Communications* (4), *Nature Metabolism*, *Science Advances*, *Cell Reports* (3) and other journals. A stable research team of postdoctoral researchers, Ph.D. and M.S. students has been formed.
