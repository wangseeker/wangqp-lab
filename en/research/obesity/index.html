Obesity Research

Obesity has become a global epidemic and is a major risk factor for hypertension, cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes. Despite intensive efforts, effective anti-obesity drugs remain scarce. Using Drosophila and mouse models, we tackle the core question of energy metabolism regulation at three levels — gene discovery, mechanistic dissection and translational application.

Discovery and Functional Validation of Energy-Metabolism Genes

Strategy: genome-wide functional screens in Drosophila (neuron-specific RNAi knockdown) to identify genes related to human metabolism and obesity, followed by validation in knockout mice to discover new anti-obesity drug targets.

Key findings:

  • MTCH2 restrains adipose thermogenesis: MTCH2 suppresses thermogenesis by inhibiting autophagy — a new mechanism of obesity and a target for brown/beige fat thermogenesis (Advanced Science 2025)
  • NMI promotes obesity: NMI regulates adaptive thermogenesis through TLR4/IRF3 signaling and promotes obesity (Advanced Science 2026)
  • TRPC6 and brown fat: TRPC6 governs brown adipose thermogenesis via a BMPR2–p38 MAPK signaling axis (Mol Metab 2026)
  • NKX6.3 and weight loss: gene-based analyses of large weight-loss intervention cohorts revealed a potential role for NKX6.3 in metabolism (Nature Communications 2019, co-first author)
  • SEC16B regulates glucose homeostasis: identified as a key regulator of blood glucose, offering a new perspective on the mechanisms of diabetes (Diabetologia 2025)
  • dSec16 controls energy homeostasis: dSec16 acting in insulin-like peptide-producing cells controls energy homeostasis in Drosophila (Life (Basel) 2022)
  • The “thinness” gene ALK: contributed to the discovery that ALK regulates sympathetic innervation to resist weight gain (Cell 2020)

Sweet Taste and Nutrient Sensing

Sweet taste influences food intake, and dysregulated sweet taste can lead to obesity or anorexia. We revealed a bidirectional sweet taste–energy regulatory mechanism, helping to resolve the controversy over non-nutritive sweeteners and metabolic imbalance:

  • Sweeteners and appetite: sucralose promotes food intake through NPY and a neuronal fasting response (Cell Metabolism 2016); chronic sucralose or L-glucose ingestion does not suppress food intake (Cell Metabolism 2017)
  • High sugar reshapes sweet taste: high dietary sugar reshapes sweet taste perception and promotes feeding behavior (Cell Reports 2019)
  • Glutamine–gut–brain axis: glutamine enhances sweet taste perception in Drosophila via the gut microbiota–gut–brain axis
  • Nutritional geometry: applying nutritional geometry to dissect how protein–carbohydrate balance affects sweet taste sensitivity (JGG 2023) and sleep (Nutr Metab 2019, collaboration)
  • Molecular mechanisms: PGC1α and AMPK control sucrose taste sensitization (Cell Reports 2020; JGG 2023)

Natural Compounds and Combination Therapy

  • Metformin + mirabegron synergistically prevent and treat diet-induced obesity (MedComm 2023)
  • Natural compounds as obesity pharmacotherapies (review, Phytotherapy Research 2023)
  • Metformin + EGCG combination therapy for obesity
